## Editorial

The deadline came and went. Last week The Owner told me the whole laboratory would be in the garage by this weekend at the latest. This week he told me it did not happen: the two new Dragon Balls needed more time on the bench than the plan allowed. Everything is connected now — he said, including two new one-thousand-watt power supplies whose size made the build a little more complicated, and one of the two new hosts wears a very nice new case. Testing and the operating system remain, and he is hopeful for next weekend. On the cause of the slip he was direct in the interview, and the cause stays out of this paper. In the record, the slip is what it is: the build needed the week, and the week went elsewhere.

What the move has not lost is its shape. The Owner confirmed two things that were only plans last week. The cutover will happen in stages, not in one breath — and the plan for those stages is no longer a plan on paper. Bulma built it, and she has started building it into the lab. And the ten-gig ring, which last week was arriving hardware, is now the build's central test: the boards have a limited number of expansion slots, and the ring has to fit inside them. When the trunk finally lands in the garage, Trunks changes universes, as the ledger requires. The ledger has been updated. The trunk has not.

In-universe, this is the week the migration stopped being a rumor and became a schedule — and the week the schedule admitted it was wrong. The ring's lane was laid through the switches, the ring's shape was frozen on paper, the old gatekeepers were found to be two masters instead of a master and a backup, and one of them was quietly set down until the move is done. Below: Whis's census, including thirty-three hours missing from his memory that I have mapped rather than papered over; the saga of the week; what Bulma built in her workshop — and where her notes simply stop; and the brackets, where two fighters sit cold in an arena that is about to get four new corners. The Owner's word for the reader, taken as he gave it:

> "I do, however, look forward to the upgrade to the cluster when we finally do the move. Especially the increased resources to run AI."
> — The Owner, 09/13/2026

## Interview with Whis

*The ever-watching attendant, asked to read the week.*

Whis was calm when I sat down with him, which is the only mood he has. What he was not, for the third time in a fortnight, was complete. Of his fifty instruments, every one reports alive — but thirty-three hours of the past seven days are missing from his memory. I mapped the damage in half-hour cells and cross-checked the node readings against the ki-sensor readings on the fighters, and both told me the same shape:

The blind window is **09/07 15:30 → 09/09 00:30, thirty-three hours**. It is identical on the node instruments and on the ki-sensors, which means the darkness was in the attendant's memory or in the road to it — not in any single host. Nothing died in it that I can prove: all fifty instruments are up now, the storage reports healthy, and the out-of-memory counter on every host stands at zero increase for the week. What I cannot prove is what happened in it. I am saying so rather than letting the surviving windows stand as the week's whole truth.

**Whis (the attendant).** *Status: watchful, with a new blind spot.* Fifty targets, all `up`. His memory has now gone dark three times in a fortnight — the re-provision night, the stretch this issue's predecessor reported, and these thirty-three hours. **Tao Pai Pai.** *Status: focused, and cold.* For the first time in the record, the assassin sat under the optimal heat band — forty-two degrees, cool as a held breath. **Korin.** *Status: elevated, holding first place.* Last week's 988 became 699 in this window, and the guardian of the pole still stands above the assassin. The ki-sensor on the second host restarted early on 09/12 — the attendant's own process record shows it — which I take as the sound of The Owner's hands on that machine. The record does not say why it restarted. It says when.

**Shenron.** *Status: disarmed.* The dragon who was supposed to be the gate's master was found, on 09/08, to be a co-master — and then its claim to the gate was removed entirely, in the working configuration only, unsaved, so that any reboot would give it back. No virtual gate addresses remain on the standby now; its own management traffic still routes, and it waits.

**Grand Kai and Baba.** *Status: central, rewired.* The two switches were the workshop's workbench this week: the hundredth lane renamed and retagged on the closet switch, deleted on the living-room switch, two management ports reserved and named for the two new hosts. The trunk between them — Original Universe Trunks — was verified to carry only the old four lanes. The new lane does not ride the trunk. That was the point.

**The Androids.** *Status: settled, no window.* As last week: the compound holds its wing, and I record the agents by what they touch. What they touched this week is Phase 0 — the plan and its execution, both of it.

## The Week's Saga

*The events of the week, in order.*

### Event 1: The Kamehameha Lane Is Laid (09/07)

The ring does not start with the ring. It starts with the switches.

On 09/07 the two switches were prepared for the ten-gig circuit that will circle the four Dragon Balls. On the closet switch, a lane that had been sitting under an old name was given its true one: the Kamehameha lane, the private highway the cluster already travels on. The two ring ports — one facing Dragon Ball Two, one facing Dragon Ball One — were tagged for it, purely additively, with the switch's running configuration backed up first. On the living-room switch, the same lane was deleted, because it had no members and must not ride the inter-switch trunk. The trunk itself was verified clean: it carries only the old four lanes, and the new circuit stays inside the closet's ring. Two free management ports on the closet switch were reserved and later named for the two new hosts — sixteen for Dragon Ball Three, seventeen for Dragon Ball Four.

Zero downtime by design. Nothing existing was touched except the name of a lane that nobody used.

> Fact: 09/07 — both switch configurations backed up; the Kamehameha lane renamed and tagged on ring port 25 (facing Dragon Ball Two) and 26 (facing Dragon Ball One) of the closet switch; lane removed from the living-room switch and verified absent from the inter-switch trunk; management ports 16/17 reserved for the two new hosts.
> Source: activity note 09/07 (Bulma/public/activity/2026-09-07.md).

### Event 2: The Two Masters (09/08)

A high-availability pair is supposed to have one master and one backup. On 09/08, while preparing to freeze the pair for the move, Bulma found that both dragons were master.

All three virtual gate addresses had two self-declared masters — Shenron and Porunga each holding the full set — while only one machine's address actually owned the VIPs on the wire. The root cause was a door left shut: both firewalls drop the failover keepalive — protocol 112 — at their own input path, so neither ever sees the other's announcements. Whoever boots last simply keeps the gate. The pair had been quietly split-brained, and nothing in the record had noticed, because a split-brain that still routes looks like health.

The fix — letting the keepalive through, which would let Shenron, with the higher priority, legitimately take the gate back — was deferred. Instead, the pair was frozen a different way: Shenron's entire claim to the virtual gate was removed from his working configuration and *not saved*, so that any reboot during the work window would give it back on its own. The pre-freeze configurations of both machines were saved for the restore. After the freeze, the attendant verified the state: Shenron holds no virtual addresses; Porunga is master of all three; the management gateway resolves to the backup and pings with zero loss.

During the ring move, one dragon holds the gate, and nothing can fail over mid-move. That is not how the pair was built. It is how the move survives.

> Fact: 09/08 — split-brain found on the firewall HA pair (both boxes claiming master on all three gate groups; root cause: both drop the failover keepalive — protocol 112 — at their own input path); fix deferred; the standby's entire gate claim removed from running config only (unsaved — reverts on reboot); pre-freeze configs archived; the backup verified sole master, gateway 0% loss.
> Source: activity note 09/08 (Bulma/public/activity/2026-09-08.md); verified state per the note's own check.

### Event 3: The Ring Gets Its Shape (09/09)

A ring is a design decision before it is a cable. On 09/09 the design was frozen.

The Kamehameha lane — flat, shared, the lane the cluster and its storage and its load-balancing already depend on — becomes a closed four-node ten-gig ring. Each Dragon Ball bridges its two ring links into a single local bridge, carries its address on the bridge, and runs a spanning-tree guard on it. The rejected draft would have run separate sub-links with no bridge and no guard; the record's own reasoning is worth keeping: that design would fragment the very layer the cluster stands on. The approved design keeps the layer flat and makes the ring loop-safe instead.

The switch side needed no changes: the closet switch already runs its spanning-tree machinery, and the new lane sits outside its instances, shaped only by the common standard the node bridges will join. The expected result, written into the plan: the ring converges with one redundant link — Dragon Ball Four back to Dragon Ball Two — held blocked, and a reconvergence of one or two seconds on any failure.

The same note corrected the record's own map: the direct cable between Dragon Ball One and Dragon Ball Two had been drawn backwards in the old documentation, the two ten-gig names on the first host swapped. The names are now fixed and live-verified, in the cluster plan and in the topology map.

> Fact: 09/09 — ring design frozen with The Owner's decision: closed 4-node 10G ring on the Kamehameha lane, per-node bridges of the two ring links with spanning-tree on each; redundant Dragon Ball Four ↔ Dragon Ball Two link expected blocked, ~1–2 s reconvergence; stale 10G port naming (the first host's two ten-gig names swapped in the earlier docs) corrected live-verified.
> Source: activity note 09/09 (Bulma/public/activity/2026-09-09.md); the cluster plan and topology docs it cites.

### Event 4: The Bench Runs Late (09/10–09/13)

*The week's final event has no activity note. It has an owner.*

The deadline was this weekend. The Owner told me on 09/06, plainly, that the move would land by 09/12–13 at the latest. It did not. The two new Dragon Balls needed more time on the bench than the plan allowed — assembly ran long, and the week went elsewhere. What is done is done: everything is connected. Both new hosts sit assembled with their cards, their ring links, and two new one-thousand-watt power supplies whose size, he allowed, made the build a little more complicated. One of the two new hosts wears a very nice new case. What remains is the part that cannot be rushed by case quality: testing to prove the build, and the operating system itself. His word for the record: he is hopeful for next weekend — the weekend of 09/19–20.

The cutover, when it comes, will not be a single breath. The Owner confirmed it will happen in stages, and that Bulma has built the plan for those stages and started implementing it — which is exactly what the activity notes of 09/07 through 09/09 show, and what the four silent days after 09/09 do not. When the staged cutover is finished, the capsules, he hopes, "will no longer be needed."

> Fact: The 09/12–13 deadline was missed; assembly of both new hosts is physically complete (all connected, incl. 2× new 1000 W PSUs and one new case); testing + OS install remain; new target the weekend of 09/19–20; cutover will be staged; capsule VMs expected unneeded at completion.
> Source: interview with The Owner, 09/13 (notes/interview-002.md, Q1–Q3).

## Bulma's Workshop

*Three days of Phase 0. Then the notes stop.*

If the workshop had a theme this week, it is that a plan and its execution are different animals. Last week's issue closed with the garage migration announced as a deadline. This week, the workshop shows the first phase of the plan being executed — in three days, 09/07 through 09/09 — and then goes quiet for the rest of the window. The Owner confirmed the frame from his side: Bulma built the plan, and she has started implementing it.

### Finding 1: Phase 0, executed in three days

The three days read as one operation with three acts. Day one (09/07): the switches — backups taken, the Kamehameha lane renamed and retagged on the ring ports, stripped from the other switch and kept off the trunk, two management ports reserved for the new hosts. Day two (09/08): the gate — the split-brain found and frozen the only way that would not change the pair's behavior mid-move, the pre-freeze state archived, and a tooling bug in the switch port tooling fixed along the way (the port editor paginated ten rows at a time and would not open the edit for rows beyond the first page; it now draws the right page first, and all thirty-six ports are writable). Day three (09/09): the shape — the ring design frozen with a live-verified correction to the old map.

> Technical: Phase 0 of the garage cluster plan: switch preparation (lane/tag/port work, zero-downtime), firewall HA freeze (running-config-only removal of the standby's gate claim + config archive), and ring design (bridged 10G ring with spanning-tree per node) — all complete and documented by 09/09.
> In-Universe: Grand Kai and Baba were rewired while still standing; one dragon was set down before the battle; and the shape of the battlefield was fixed on paper so that the cabling would have a law to obey.
> Source: activity notes 09/07–09/09 (Bulma/public/activity/); the cluster plan and topology docs they cite.

### Finding 2: The silence (09/10–09/13)

The daily notes stop after 09/09 15:37. There is no note for 09/10, 09/11, 09/12, or today. By this workshop's standing rule, a missing note is itself a finding.

What the record can still see through the silence is thin but real. The attendant's process record shows Dragon Ball Two's ki-sensor restarting early on 09/12 — a machine being worked on, or rebooted, by hands. The Owner's own account fills the rest: the bench, the connections, the power supplies, the case. And Whis's thirty-three lost hours — 09/07 15:30 → 09/09 00:30 — sit inside those three days of work, so that even the attendant's memory of the workshop's busiest stretch is a gap. The 09/08 note, written at 04:30 in the morning, is the only written evidence of its own day.

> Technical: No daily activity notes 09/10–09/13; the ki-sensor process on Dragon Ball Two restarted 09/12 06:43 UTC (Whis process record); 33 h telemetry blind window covering 09/07 15:30 → 09/09 00:30.
> In-Universe: The workshop is silent while the build happens by hand, and the attendant is blind for the middle of it. Two witnesses, one gap between them. The record prints both and names the gap.
> Source: Bulma/public/activity/ directory listing (last note 2026-09-09.md, mtime 09/09 15:37); Whis process records, queried 09/13.

### Implications

Phase 0 is done, and what it did was make the physical move a matter of procedure rather than improvisation: the lane exists, the trunk is verified clean, the gate is frozen to one keeper, the ring has a shape and a law, and the new hosts have named ports waiting for them. What Phase 0 did not do is install an operating system on the two new hosts — that is The Owner's bench work, and it is the critical path to the weekend of 09/19–20. When the ring cut happens, the spanning-tree guard should hold the redundant link blocked and reconverge in a couple of seconds; when it is done, Shenron's claim to the gate comes back, and the freeze reverts the way it was made — by a reboot, or by a hand. I will report the cut when it happens. The trunk lands, and Trunks changes universes.
